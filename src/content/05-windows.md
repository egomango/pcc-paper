## Doors open and close

Practitioners know timing matters. Two identical products, months apart, can land very differently. The folklore answer — right place, right time, read the tea leaves — gestures at the phenomenon without naming the mechanism.

The mechanism is structural. A door opens for a new product when the customer's disturbance landscape shifts and the incumbent regulator can't cover the new rows at viable cost. The opening is the gap between what the incumbent can still handle and what the world now demands. If your product closes that gap, you have structural entry. If it doesn't, no amount of absolute quality on rows the incumbent already covers will carry you through.

The diagnostic is simple. Look where incumbents are straining at the edges of their coverage, not where they're executing poorly. Execution noise is competitive weather. Strain at the edges is structural opening. They look similar from the outside and reward very different strategies.

What this framework doesn't do is predict which shifts will happen next — that requires external knowledge of technology, regulation, and markets the framework doesn't internalise. What it does is give you the structural condition an entrant has to satisfy once the shift is visible.

Doors are read, not waited for.
