## Doors open and close

Practitioners know timing matters. Two identical products, months apart, can land very differently. No framework explains why beyond folklore — right place, right time, read the tea leaves.

There's a structural account. A door opens for a new product when the customer's disturbance landscape shifts and the incumbent regulator can't cover the new rows at viable cost. The opening is the gap between what the incumbent can still handle and what the world now demands. If your product closes that gap, you have structural entry. If it doesn't, no amount of absolute quality on rows the incumbent already covers will carry you through.

The AWS transition is the same shape. Geo-distributed access and elastic analytics weren't bugs in on-prem. They were new rows in the disturbance landscape that appeared as the enterprise customer's world drifted. The incumbent was executing fine. The landscape had moved past its coverage.

The diagnostic is simple. Look where incumbents are straining at the edges of their coverage, not where they're executing poorly. Execution noise is competitive weather. Strain at the edges is structural opening. They look similar from the outside and they reward very different strategies.

What this framework does not do is forecast which shifts will happen next. That requires external knowledge of technology, regulation and markets it doesn't internalise. What it tells you is the structural condition an entrant has to satisfy once the shift is visible.

Doors are read, not waited for.
