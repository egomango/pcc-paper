## Doors open and close

Practitioners know timing matters. Two identical products, months apart, can land very differently. The folklore answer — right place, right time, read the tea leaves — gestures at the phenomenon without naming the mechanism.

The mechanism is structural. A door opens for a new product when the customer's disturbance landscape shifts and the incumbent regulator can't cover the newly load-bearing rows at viable cost. The opening is the gap between what the incumbent can still handle and what the world now demands. If your product closes that gap, you have structural entry. If it doesn't, no amount of absolute quality on rows the incumbent already covers will carry you through.

The cleanest worked case is enterprise IT through the cloud transition. Through the 1990s and early 2000s, the incumbent was the on-premises datacentre, fitted to stable internal workloads and scheduled batch. Through the late 2000s and 2010s, the landscape drifted: spiky event-driven demand, geo-distributed access, and elastic analytical workloads became load-bearing. The on-premises regulator couldn't cover the new rows at viable cost. Public cloud didn't win by beating on-premises at what on-premises already did well — it won by closing the rows the shift had opened. "Lift-and-shift" migrations that moved stable workloads into the cloud without capturing the elastic cells paid destruction cost and got nothing in return.

The diagnostic is simple. Look where incumbents are straining at the edges of their coverage, not where they're executing poorly. Execution noise is competitive weather. Strain at the edges is structural opening. They look similar from outside and reward very different strategies.

What this framework doesn't do is predict which shifts will happen next — that needs external knowledge of technology, regulation, and markets the framework doesn't internalise. What it does is give you the structural condition an entrant has to satisfy once the shift is visible. Falsifier across cohorts: once absolute quality is controlled, entrant success should track whether the new product closes the cells the shift opened. If it doesn't, the claim fails.

Doors are read, not waited for.
